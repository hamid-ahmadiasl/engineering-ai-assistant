Engineering AI Assistant

A knowledge-driven assistant for pressure equipment and heat exchangers. Engineers ask in their own words and get an answer that stays tied to a clause, a page, and a revision. The engineer still decides.

The problem

On a heat exchanger or a pressure vessel, the answer to a simple question is scattered. A project specification, a datasheet, a vendor offer, and a code edition each say something slightly different. Finding the page is slow. Treating the strictest number as the winner is also wrong: the contract already says which document governs.

General chat tools will answer anyway, without the project file and without a page number. That is not usable next to a nameplate. This assistant is decision support. It retrieves, it labels what is a requirement and what is only a reading of the file, and it stops before the design decision.

What the prototype does

Global questions

Background notes on design pressure, TEMA letters, and the different jobs of the equipment standards. The notes are original demo text, not a licensed code.

Project files

Upload a PDF or an Excel datasheet. Text and tables are indexed with the file, page, and revision, in a store that other projects cannot see.

Evidence

Every answer cites the source, clause, page, and revision, and marks an explicit requirement, a recommendation, or an interpretation.

Conflicts

Specification, datasheet, and vendor offer are compared with the project’s own hierarchy. A higher vendor number does not automatically govern.

Troubleshooting

A plain-language problem returns considerations and limits, next to the project constraints such as design pressure and MDMT.

Accounts

Sign-in, invite codes, and a monthly credit balance. The public demo can offer a short anonymous trial and keep uploads closed.

Architecture

Two stores stay separate: one global knowledge base, and one directory per project. They are combined only at the moment of the question. An orchestration step applies the document hierarchy before any wording is produced.

Research questions

RQ1

Can a hybrid of lexical and vector search, over a global store and a separate project store, answer engineering questions from those documents rather than from the model’s memory?

RQ2

Can each answer show its source, clause or section, page, and revision or edition, and keep an explicit requirement distinct from a recommendation and from an interpretation?

RQ3

Can conflicts between a project specification, a datasheet, and a vendor offer be explained with the project’s document hierarchy, instead of a rule that the strictest number wins?

RQ4

Are the citations reliable? A small fixed set of questions checks that the cited document, clause, and revision are the governing source. The script is eval/rq4_traceability.py in the repository.
